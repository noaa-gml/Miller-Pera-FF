# GridFED v. CDIAC: where the Miller-Pera gap comes from (2026-10-07)

Hand-written note on `outputs/national_comparison.md` and `.png` (generated by
`compare_national.py`). It complements `outputs/product_comparison.md`, which compares the
global totals and the spatial patterns. All values are in TgC yr⁻¹ over 1993–2022, the
CDIAC years. Miller-Pera is `gml_ff_co2_2026b_assumed`; GridFED v2025.1 is gross
(`TOTAL − CEMENT_CARBONATION`).

**Method.** Both grids are summed over the GISS 1° country mask that `ff_country.py`
grids with, and both are split into national and ocean parts.

- **Mask gate.** Miller-Pera reproduces CDIAC within 0.30 % for the ten largest emitters
  in every year, against a 1 % tolerance. The gate failed twice while this was being
  written, on a missing time axis and on province codes. Both bugs are fixed; the
  known-bad mask is in `tests/test_compare_national.py`.
- **Off-mask cells.** GridFED is built at 0.1°, so some of its national emissions sit in
  1° cells the mask calls ocean. Each such cell is given to its nearest country.
- **Cross-check.** The country rows are checked against the GCB 2024 national table
  (ungridded, so no mask is involved). It is one vintage older than the GCB 2025 totals
  behind GridFED v2025.1.

## Findings

1. **The global gap is two terms, and they move in opposite directions.**

   | | gap | national, GridFED − CDIAC | ocean, GridFED bunkers − Miller-Pera ocean cells |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 2000 | +194 (+2.9 %) | +186 | +8 |
   | 2010 | +178 (+2.0 %) | +178 | −0 |
   | 2022 | −74 (−0.7 %) | +12 | −87 |
   | mean 1993–2022 | +136 (+1.6 %) | +171 (+2.2 %) | −35 |

2. **The national excess is mostly cement.** Means by fuel: COAL +36, OIL +24, GAS and
   flaring +27, CEMENT +80. The cement term grows from +68 (2000) to +94 (2022). By 2022
   the three fuels have turned negative (COAL −57, OIL −15, GAS −14). The fuels are
   matched as labelled: GridFED has no separate flaring or "other" field, so GCB's
   non-cement process CO₂ lands wherever GridFED puts it.
3. **Most of the post-2012 change is a CDIAC accounting step on the ocean side, not
   convergence.** Miller-Pera puts CDIAC's global total minus the sum of nations on ocean
   cells.
   - That residual jumps from 324 (2012) to 397 (2013).
   - Since 2013 the ocean cells carry 82–147 more than GridFED's bunkers in every year
     except 2021 (−40).
   - This is what pulls the global gap from about +2 % to about 0 from 2016 on.
4. **North America is 5–6 % low in Miller-Pera, and two methods agree.**

   | USA + Canada + Mexico | 2000 | 2010 | 2022 |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | gridded GridFED vs Miller-Pera | +6.4 % | +5.9 % | +5.2 % |
   | ungridded GCB 2024 vs CDIAC | +6.2 % | +6.1 % | +5.7 % |

   - The USA alone is +5.3 % gridded and +7.3 % in the GCB table for 2022, nearly all of
     it OIL (+64 of +69).
   - Canada is −4.7 % in the 2022 GCB table.
   - This is the bias CT's NA nest inherits from CDIAC-based Miller-Pera.
5. **Other large emitters, 2022, gridded vs GCB table:**

   | country | gridded | GCB table |
   |---|---|---|
   | Russia | −18.5 % | −7.6 % |
   | China | −1.7 % | −2.5 % |
   | India | −3.2 % | −2.5 % |
   | Germany | +14 % | +9.7 % |
   | Indonesia | −9 % | −15 % |

   Where the two disagree on size, as for Russia, the gridded number includes border cells
   traded with neighbours: Kazakhstan, Lithuania (Kaliningrad) and Kyrgyzstan come out
   positive. Small countries in the gridded table (Lithuania, Kyrgyzstan, Bahrain,
   Trinidad) are mask artefacts. Read regions, not small countries.
6. **Placement differs from totals.** 8.8–9.0 % of GridFED's national emissions (606 in
   2000, 883 in 2022) sit on 1° cells the mask calls ocean: coastal cities and domestic
   shipping. Miller-Pera puts that mass on the nearest land cells. This matters for coastal
   sites, separately from any totals decision.

## What this does not settle

- **Shared lineage.** GridFED and Miller-Pera both use EDGAR spatial patterns, so their
  spatial agreement in `product_comparison.md` is not independent.
- **GCB vintage.** The national cross-check uses GCB 2024, while GridFED v2025.1 uses
  GCB 2025 totals.
- **The ocean term in finding 3.** Why CDIAC's global-minus-national residual steps up in
  2013 (bunkers or a statistical difference) is a question for CDIAC at AppState
  (Erb & Marland), alongside the open coal question.
